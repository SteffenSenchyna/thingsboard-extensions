• product lines and markets group the sites. Every site belongs to one product line and one market.
  • A product line can have sub product lines under it, to any depth. A site in a sub product line also counts under every product line above it.
  • An application runs at the sites that have a device of one of its device profiles. A site doesn't show up in an application's dashboard unless the application runs there.
  • A site holds its assets, gateways and devices. An asset can have several devices associated with it, and a gateway keeps track of the devices that connect through it.
  • A user has a scope of their own, the part of the organization they work in, and can take many roles: their own, and one with each access group they join.
  • A user is given one role and the sites they have access to, and belongs to that role's default groups for them: the region's for every site, a market's for all of its sites, else each site's.
  • A user can also join other access groups within their sites, adding each group's role and what it gives.
  • Each site counts its users: those whose own scope is it. A product line or a market counts every user whose scope reaches any of it: a user whose scope spans several product lines or markets counts under each.
  • A role is a template for access groups: each access group takes one role, which sets what its users may do.
  • Its actions say what they may do with what they reach: view, change or control devices, view or change assets and alarms, view dashboards, and see or manage the group's members.
  • It applies to parts of the organization: the whole region, and every or some of the product lines, markets and sites. Each part it applies to gets a default group of the role: saving the role makes any that are missing, renames them with it, and offers to remove the empty ones where it no longer applies.
  • A role still in use, by a group with members, a hand-made group or a user, can't be deleted.
  • An access group gives its users access to part of the organization: where (its scope) and how (its role).
  • Its scope is one of: the whole region, a product line, a market (optionally just one product line's sites in it) or a single site. The group reaches every site inside it, with every device and asset there.
  • The applications running at its sites open for its users: their dashboards, and their entries in Home's menu.
  • Its role sets what its users may do with them.
  • Every market, product line and site has default groups, one for each role that applies to it, made, renamed and removed along with it. A user belongs to those of their role for the sites they have access to.
  • Each application represents a dashboard: users with a site it runs at in their scope see it in Home's menu, and picking it there opens that dashboard.
  • An application runs at the sites that have a device of one of its device profiles, and only those sites show up in its dashboard.
  • An application is associated with device profiles and asset profiles: the kinds of devices and assets it works with.
  • access groups whose scope holds a site it runs at open its dashboard. What their users reach is their scope's: every device and asset at its sites, whatever the application.
  • product lines group the sites. Every site belongs to one product line, and a product line holds any number of sites.
  • A product line can have sub product lines under it, to any depth. A site in a sub product line also counts under every product line above it.
  • product lines and markets are two separate ways of grouping the same sites: each site belongs to one of each, so a product line's sites can be in several markets.
  • users and access groups can be scoped to a product line: they then reach every site in it and in its sub product lines. A scope can also pair a product line with a market, reaching only that market's sites in the product line.
  • markets group the sites by region. Every site belongs to one market, and a market holds any number of sites.
  • markets and product lines are two separate ways of grouping the same sites: each site belongs to one of each, so a market's sites can come from several product lines.
  • users and access groups can be scoped to a market: they then reach every site in it. A scope can also pair a market with a product line, reaching only that product line's sites in the market.
